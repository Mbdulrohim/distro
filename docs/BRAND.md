## Positioning

Distro is infrastructure, not a consumer app. The audience is technical and semi-technical operators — founders, DAO ops, community managers, payroll teams — who need distribution to be correct, scheduled, and boring rather than flashy. Voice reflects that: precise, trustworthy, no hype.

The strategic claim: **the distribution layer teams trust with payroll, because it can't miss a run.**

## Voice

- Clear over clever. No crypto-hype language ("moon", "degen", excessive emoji).
- Confident about correctness — this product moves real money on a schedule; copy never sounds casual about that.
- Terse UI copy; longer explanation lives in docs/tooltips, not crammed into buttons.
- Name the risk plainly. Distributions are irreversible; copy says so rather than softening it.

## Vocabulary

Consistency matters more than variety — pick one word per concept and never drift:

| Use           | Not                                |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| distribution  | campaign, batch, blast             |
| recipients    | claimants, users, holders          |
| execute       | run, send, fire                    |
| scheduled     | queued, pending, timed             |
| paid / failed | success/error, complete/incomplete |

"Campaign" and "claim" are legacy vocabulary from a superseded model — they should appear nowhere in product copy.
